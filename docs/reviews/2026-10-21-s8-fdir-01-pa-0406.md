## 2. Findings

### Blocker

**F-01 — The panic path is documented as "bounded and lock-free", but in the flight wiring it can block before the reset and therefore prevent it.**
`pal_posix.c:546-558` claims no lock is taken and everything is bounded. `FF_PalPanic` performs `fprintf`/`fflush(stderr)` (`:568-569`), then the notifier (`:571-573`), then `FF_PalWatchdogReset()` (`:575`). The flight wiring installs `FF_LinkSend` (`src/main/fsw_main.c:98`), which on RTEMS reaches the UART link's blocking `write()` on a blocking descriptor (`link_uart.c:88`, `:107-112`). A panicking task can therefore block in stdio or in `write()` and never reach the reset, contradicting SRS-REQ-074 ("failure or absence of that notification shall not prevent the reset from being enforced") and ADR-0021. Separately, `FF_PusNextTmCounter()` is a non-atomic read-modify-write invoked on this path (`pus.c:107-112`, `:192`). NC-EXT-123 registers only the *wording* of the timing bound; the blocking-prevents-reset consequence is registered nowhere.
**Corrective action:** register as a safety NC and escalate; either bound the write (non-blocking/timeout) and drop or defer the stdio log, or drive the reset from a path independent of the writer; otherwise the requirements authority must explicitly accept the residual risk and the code comment must stop claiming boundedness.
